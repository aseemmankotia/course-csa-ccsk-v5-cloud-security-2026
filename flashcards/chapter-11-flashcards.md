## Chapter 11: Domain 12: Related Technologies and Strategies — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Zero Trust core principle in one phrase? | Never trust, always verify — authenticate and authorize every request regardless of network location. Exam tip: rejects implicit trust based on perimeter/network location. |
| 2 | NIST SP 800-207: what does PEP stand for and what does it do? | Policy Enforcement Point — enables, monitors, and terminates connections between subject and resource per the decision from the Policy Decision Point. Exam tip: PEP enforces, it does not decide. |
| 3 | NIST SP 800-207: what is the Policy Engine (PE)? | Component of the Policy Decision Point that makes the grant/deny access decision based on policy and a trust algorithm evaluating contextual signals. Exam tip: distinguish PE (decides) from PEP (enforces). |
| 4 | SASE stands for and combines what two categories? | Secure Access Service Edge — converges SD-WAN networking with cloud-delivered security services. Exam tip: it's a convergence architecture, not a single product. |
| 5 | Name the 4 security services typically bundled in SASE's security stack. | SWG (Secure Web Gateway), CASB (Cloud Access Security Broker), FWaaS (Firewall as a Service), ZTNA (Zero Trust Network Access). Exam tip: SASE = SD-WAN + these delivered from the cloud edge. |
| 6 | What is the 'data leakage into models' risk in GenAI security? | Sensitive or proprietary data submitted as prompts or training input can be memorized or exposed in model outputs. Exam tip: mitigate via classification, redaction, and output filtering before use. |
| 7 | Define prompt injection. | An attack where malicious input manipulates an LLM into ignoring its original instructions, leaking data, or taking unintended actions. Exam tip: conceptually similar to SQL injection but targets natural-language instruction parsing. |
| 8 | What is 'model supply chain risk' in AI/GenAI security? | Risk that third-party pretrained models, datasets, or plugins are tampered with, poisoned, or contain hidden malicious behavior. Exam tip: mitigate with signature/hash validation and provenance checks. |
| 9 | What is training data poisoning? | Attacker deliberately injects malicious or biased samples into training data to manipulate model behavior/output. Exam tip: a subtype of model supply chain risk affecting ML pipeline integrity. |
| 10 | Zero Trust: define 'least privilege' in this context. | Grant only the minimum access necessary for a specific task/session, continuously reassessed rather than granted permanently. Exam tip: pairs with continuous verification, not a one-time grant. |
| 11 | Zero Trust: what does 'continuous verification' mean vs traditional perimeter security? | Trust is never permanent — every request is re-evaluated using real-time context (identity, device posture, location, behavior) instead of trusting a session after initial login. Exam tip: contrasts with 'trust once, trust forever' perimeter model. |
| 12 | Why are Zero Trust, SASE, and AI/GenAI security called 'cross-cutting' in CCSK Domain 12? | Their principles and controls intersect with nearly every other CCSK domain (IAM, network security, data security, incident response) rather than being isolated to one domain. Exam tip: expect questions linking Domain 12 concepts back to earlier domains. |
