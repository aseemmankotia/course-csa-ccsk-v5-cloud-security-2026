## Chapter 4: Domain 5: Identity and Access Management — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | SAML vs OIDC: data format and typical use case? | SAML = XML-based assertions, browser redirect flows, common in enterprise web SSO. OIDC = JSON/JWT over REST, built for mobile/native apps and API-driven auth. Exam tip: pick OIDC for modern/mobile, SAML for legacy enterprise SSO. |
| 2 | What is OpenID Connect (OIDC) built on top of? | OAuth 2.0. OIDC adds an identity layer (ID token/JWT) on top of OAuth 2.0's authorization framework. Exam tip: OAuth 2.0 alone is authorization, not authentication — OIDC is what adds authentication. |
| 3 | RBAC vs ABAC — when to use each? | RBAC assigns permissions via static predefined roles — simple, good for stable org structures. ABAC evaluates dynamic attributes (user, resource, environment context) — needed for fine-grained, context-aware decisions. Exam tip: 'dynamic/contextual' in a scenario = ABAC. |
| 4 | Federation vs Directory Synchronization — core difference? | Federation = trust relationship allowing acceptance of assertions from another IdP, no credential copying. Sync = periodic replication of identity data/attributes between directories. Exam trap: these are often confused as the same thing. |
| 5 | Three classic MFA factor categories? | 1) Something you know (password/PIN), 2) Something you have (token/phone), 3) Something you are (biometric). Exam tip: two passwords ≠ MFA — must span different categories. |
| 6 | What is Just-in-Time (JIT) privileged access? | Time-bound elevation of privileges granted only for the duration needed to complete an approved task, then automatically revoked. Core capability of PAM solutions to eliminate standing admin access. |
| 7 | Why are 'standing privileges' a security risk in cloud environments? | Permanently active admin rights expand the attack surface — if the account/credential is compromised, the attacker has continuous elevated access. PAM/JIT mitigates this by granting access only when needed. |
| 8 | What is 'entitlement sprawl'? | Accumulation of excessive, unused, or redundant permissions across users/services over time, often from incomplete deprovisioning or over-provisioning. Increases breach blast radius; mitigated by periodic entitlement reviews and least privilege enforcement. |
| 9 | What are the three stages of the identity lifecycle (joiner-mover-leaver)? | Joiner: account provisioning on hire. Mover: entitlement adjustment on role change. Leaver: deprovisioning on termination. Exam tip: broken 'leaver' process is the most commonly tested lifecycle failure (orphaned privileged accounts). |
| 10 | What core components make up a SAML assertion? | Subject (who), Conditions (validity constraints, e.g. time window/audience), and Statements (authentication, attribute, or authorization decision data). Exam tip: know that SAML assertions are XML-signed by the IdP. |
| 11 | What is the main challenge of cross-provider identity federation in multi-cloud? | Inconsistent attribute schemas, trust configuration, and protocol support (SAML vs OIDC vs proprietary) across providers make establishing a single unified trust fabric complex — often requiring an identity broker/hub pattern. |
| 12 | PAM best practice for privileged credentials? | Vault credentials centrally, broker sessions (no direct credential exposure to user), auto-rotate after each checkout, and record/audit sessions. Exam tip: shared spreadsheets or static shared admin passwords are the wrong-answer pattern. |
